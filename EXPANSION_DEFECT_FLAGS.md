# Expansion Defect Flags — applied-diminished chord_tones (downstream research flag, fork only)

This file is a downstream research flag maintained on the JimPlamondon fork of ms3 (the Python parser for MuseScore files developed by Johannes Hentschel for the DCML, the Digital and Cognitive Musicology Lab at EPFL). No upstream issue or Pull Request has been filed. The producer of the `chord_tones` columns has now been established (see "Root-cause verdict" below): ms3's expansion arithmetic faithfully implements the DCML harmony annotation standard, and the defect family lives in the corpus labels, not in ms3 code.

## The defect family

In Distant Listening Corpus (DLC, the DCML's multi-composer corpus of harmony-annotated scores) data parsed with ms3, the machine expansion of applied-diminished labels — the step that turns a Roman-numeral label such as `viio7/V` into the `chord_tones` column's concrete pitch list — can mis-spell the leading tone of the tonicized degree. An applied (or "tonicized") chord is one heard relative to a scale degree other than the tonic, written with a `relativeroot` after the slash (the `/V` part); `viio` is the leading-tone diminished chord, whose root is the raised note a semitone below the tonicized degree. The observed defect: for viio-family labels carrying a `relativeroot`, `chord_tones` can encode that leading tone with its sharpening dropped (the unraised note, 7 steps flat on the line of fifths, hereafter "-7 of the correct spelling" — so the fix is +7) or over-raised (7 steps sharp, so the fix is -7). Either way the encoded note sits a chromatic semitone — the interval between a note and its own sharpened or flattened form, e.g. B versus B# — off the engraved staff, in one direction or the other.

## Scale of the finding (audit of 2026-08-06)

- Positive control: 6 rows in Beethoven's String Quartet op. 131, movement ii (the op131 control case), where owner-adjudicated corrections of exactly this shape were already accepted and were reproduced field-for-field by the audit machinery.
- 129 rows where the +7 correction (raising the dropped sharpening) matches the engraved staff (the "+7 hits").
- 93 rows where the mirror -7 correction (lowering an over-raised member) matches the engraved staff (the "-7 hits").
- 28 additional hits in the augmented-sixth family (Ger/Fr/It labels, the chords built on the interval of an augmented sixth), showing the same dropped-sharpening signature.
- 5 direction flips eye-verified against published engravings on 2026-08-06: machine tiebreaks first chose +7, but the printed page shows the -7 spelling literally and the +7 respelling nowhere.

## The five eye-verified loci

- `beethoven_piano_sonatas/harmonies/07-1.harmonies.tsv` mc=47;mn=46 (mc is the file's raw measure count, mn the printed measure number), label `viio6/V` (the figures 6, 7, 65, 43 are figured-bass inversion shorthand): engraved B natural; corrected members B, F#, D#.
- `beethoven_piano_sonatas/harmonies/07-1.harmonies.tsv` mc=227;mn=225, label `viio6/V`: engraved D natural (tied); corrected members D, A, F# — a plain major triad under a diminished-implying label.
- `ABC/harmonies/n06op18-6_04.harmonies.tsv` (ABC is the Annotated Beethoven Corpus of string quartets) mc=36;mn=36, label `viio7(6)/V`: engraved B-flat with an explicit flat sign; corrected members Bb, C, F#, D#.
- `liszt_pelerinage/harmonies/160.06_Vallee_dObermann.harmonies.tsv` mc=109;mn=105, label `viio7(4)/V`: engraved B natural; corrected members E, B, C#, F##.
- `liszt_pelerinage/harmonies/162.02_Canzone.harmonies.tsv` mc=56;mn=56, label `viio65(4)/V`: engraved D-flat by the key signature; corrected members Gb, Db, Eb, A.

## Displacement classes: one established, and a second proposed and ruled out (audit of 2026-08-07)

A displacement class is the fixed number of chain-of-fifths positions separating every member of a machine expansion from the note the engraved staff writes in its place. This document establishes exactly one such class: the seven-position class, the chromatic semitone described above, in both its directions (the +7 and -7 hits). No twelve-position class, the enharmonic comma (the distance between two spellings that sound the same pitch on a keyboard, for example C-sharp against D-flat), is established here; where the corpus registry records "enharmonic divergence" it is recording a per-row verdict about a particular chord, not a uniform displacement family.

A five-position class, the diatonic semitone (the distance between two adjacent letter-names a semitone apart, for example E-flat against F-flat), was proposed on 2026-08-06 as a third, never-audited family. It has been tested at the locus it was proposed for and ruled out. That locus is the Annotated Beethoven Corpus file `ABC/harmonies/n07op59-1_01.harmonies.tsv` at mc=163;mn=163, onset 1/2, label `bvii6`, local key F minor. The staff there sounds G-flat, D-flat, E-flat and B-flat, containing a plain E-flat minor triad in first inversion. The shipped `chord_tones` value is "-12, -8, -9" in positions relative to the local tonic F, which spells G-double-flat, E-double-flat and B-double-flat. Every member is therefore seven positions flat of the staff, not five; five positions flat would give F-flat, A-double-flat and C-flat, which the corpus does not contain. The mechanism is the one documented above seen in mirror image: the label lowers the numeral `vii` with a flat while the local key is already minor, where the seventh degree is the natural subtonic, so the degree is lowered a second time, and calling `features2tpcs` with numeral `vii` instead of `bvii` returns the staff spelling exactly. The case is registered as DLC-0203 in the corpus fork, refining the earlier and coarser flag DLC-0012 for the same chord. No new displacement class is claimed.

## Enharmonic re-spelling: the guidelines are silent on overriding a notated spelling (finding of 2026-08-07)

Distinct from any displacement family, some expansions disagree with the engraved staff while displacing no member at all: the expansion preserves every sounding pitch and changes only how each note is written. The worked case is Medtner, Skazka op. 48 no. 1, printed measures 150 and 428 (raw measure counts 151 and 429), onset 1/4, label `Ger7/bV`. The staff sounds C, D, F-sharp and A, a plain dominant seventh chord on D in third inversion. The corpus parses that label into numeral `vii`, form `o`, figbass `7`, changes `b3` and relativeroot `V/bV`, which is precisely the German-sixth equivalence both the standard and ms3 implement, and the resulting expansion re-spells the same four pitch classes as E-double-flat, G-flat, B-double-flat and C. The arithmetic is faithful; what is open is the annotator's choice of the flattened-fifth frame, which is what forces the re-spelling.

In the sections consulted for the root-cause research above — "Roman numerals", "Relative key" and "Augmented sixth chords" — the Harmonic Annotation Guidelines state how a label is built and how it expands, but contain no rule about when a composer's notated spelling may be overridden. Overriding a notated spelling is therefore an unguided annotator judgement rather than a rule violation, and it is not a code defect; it is also what pushes such chords outside the naming systems that consume this data. These two cases are registered as DLC-0205 and DLC-0206 in the corpus fork as annotation questions rather than as defects. Flagging the confusability of the guidelines themselves would need a fork of DCMLab/standards, which we do not currently maintain, as already noted under "Where the flag belongs".

## Root-cause verdict (code-provenance research of 2026-08-06)

Verdict: NOT an ms3 code bug. ms3's expansion arithmetic is a faithful implementation of the DCML harmony annotation standard, verified against the standard's own text; the defect-family rows are places where the corpus label, read strictly per the standard, does not denote the chord engraved on the staff — i.e. annotation errors (or at least label-vs-staff disagreements) in the corpus, of exactly the shape the standard itself warns about as a "common mistake".

### The code chain that produces chord_tones, root, bass_note

- Entry point: `expand_labels` in `src/ms3/expand_dcml.py` (line 59) — called by `ms3 extract -X` / `Score.mscx.expanded()` — which runs `split_labels` (regex parse of the label into numeral/form/figbass/changes/relativeroot), `replace_special` (`src/ms3/expand_dcml.py` line 445: the table `It -> viio6(b3)/V`, `Ger -> viio65(b3)/V`, `Fr -> V43(b5)/V`, identical to the standard's own equivalence table), `propagate_keys`, then `compute_chord_tones`.
- `compute_chord_tones` in `src/ms3/transformations.py` (line 374) calls `features2tpcs` per row with `minor=localkey_is_minor` and the default `key="C"` (= 0), which is why the TSV values are fifths relative to the LOCAL tonic; `bass_note` is simply `chord_tones[0]` (`src/ms3/transformations.py` circa line 484).
- `features2tpcs` in `src/ms3/utils/functions.py` (line 5450) is where the arithmetic happens. For a label with a `relativeroot`, lines 5541-5560 resolve the tonicized degree (`resolve_relative_keys`, `roman_numeral2fifths`), take the target's mode from its CASE (`rel_minor = str_is_minor(resolved)`, line 5543: lowercase = minor), and recurse with the transposed key and that mode.
- The decisive lines are 5573-5577: the scale array for a minor context is `(0, 2, -3, -1, 1, -4, -2)` — NATURAL minor, so degree vii = -2 fifths = the unraised subtonic — versus `(0, 2, 4, -1, 1, 3, 5)` for major, where degree vii = +5 = the leading tone. A `#` prefix on the numeral adds +7 fifths (line 5580, `root = tpcs[0] + 7 * root_alteration`). Consequently `viio7/ii` lands on the unraised subtonic of the tonicized minor degree, while `#viio7/ii` lands on its leading tone; conversely lines 5562-5567 silently strip `#vii` down to `vii` in major contexts (a change documented in ms3's changelog as far back as version 0.4.x: "interpret all #vii in major contexts as vii when computing chord tones").
- Version equivalence: the shipped Distant Listening Corpus harmonies TSVs carry `"creator": {"name": "ms3", "softwareVersion": "2.5.4"}` in their `*.harmonies.resource.json` sidecars; the decisive lines are verbatim-identical at tag v2.5.4 (there: lines 5510 and 5522 of `src/ms3/utils/functions.py`) and at this fork's HEAD (2.6.3-based), and the reproduction below regenerates the shipped values field-for-field.

### Reproduction receipt (op131 control case)

Row: the Annotated Beethoven Corpus file `ABC/harmonies/n14op131_02.harmonies.tsv`, mc=63 (printed measure 61), globalkey D, localkey V (= A major), label `viio7/ii`. The tonicized degree ii of A major is B minor; the engraved chord is A#-C#-E-G, which relative to the local tonic A is fifths (7, 4, 1, -2). The shipped row instead has `chord_tones` "0, -3, -6, -9" (= A-C-Eb-Gb), root 0, bass_note 0 — every member 7 fifths flat, the +7 signature. Mechanism, each step run against this fork's actual code: `resolve_relative_keys("ii", False)` returns `ii`; `str_is_minor` marks the target minor; `roman_numeral2fifths` transposes by +2; the recursion looks numeral vii up in the natural-minor array and gets offset -2, so root = 2 - 2 = 0 (A natural, the local tonic itself); the diminished-seventh stack (root, root-3, root-6, root-9) then yields exactly (0, -3, -6, -9). Calling `features2tpcs(numeral="#vii", ...)` with the same arguments yields (7, 4, 1, -2) — the engraved spelling. Parsing the engraved score `ABC/MS3/n14op131_02.mscx` end-to-end with `ms3.Score(...).mscx.expanded()` regenerates the identical defective row, closing the chain from staff to TSV.

### Why this is the standard's convention, not an ms3 invention

The canonical home of the standard is the DCMLab/standards repository (https://github.com/DCMLab/standards), published as "Harmonic Annotation Guidelines" (version 2.3.0 at the time of this research) at https://dcmlab.github.io/standards. Its Annotation Reference page (build/html/reference/reference.html) states, in the section "Roman numerals": "The root distances of iii III vi VI vii VII from the tonic are major intervals in major keys and minor intervals in minor keys" and "For altered scale degrees, prefix the numeral with b or #". In the section "Relative key" it works the exact example: "viio7/V is not spelled #viio7/V although the bass is altered. The reason is that it is the natural 7th scale degree in the key of V (D major)", and then flags the confusion explicitly: "Common mistake: Since the root of viio/V has an accidental, novice annotators are often tempted to write #viio/V. This is incorrect because the leading tone is scale degree 7 (not #7) in major. These are all correct: I.viio/V, i.viio/V, I.#viio/v, i.#viio/v." The last sentence is decisive for our family: with a MINOR target (lowercase after the slash), the sharp is REQUIRED (`#viio/v`), so a corpus label `viio7/ii` that intends the engraved leading-tone chord is mis-annotated per the standard, and ms3's unraised expansion of it is the standard's defined reading. The same page's "Augmented sixth chords" section defines It6/Ger6/Fr6 by the identical equivalence table ms3 implements, so the augmented-sixth expansions are likewise standard-faithful in themselves; the audit's augmented-sixth hits therefore need per-row adjudication in the corpus registry (they may involve enharmonic engraving choices or label variants rather than this arithmetic).

### Where the flag belongs

Because the defect family lives in corpus labels, the primary flag home is our corpus fork (repository `JimPlamondon/distant_listening_corpus`, branch `agent/corpus-annotation-corrections`, file `corrections/candidates.tsv`), which we already maintain. The standard's own home, DCMLab/standards, is a repository we do NOT currently fork; if we ever want to flag the confusability of the convention itself (the standard's "common mistake" note shows the designers knew annotators trip on exactly this), a fork of DCMLab/standards would be the right artifact for that separate, design-level flag. This ms3 fork's flag (this file) stands only as the pointer connecting the two.

## Status

Research flag on our fork only — filed downstream for our own chord-naming work, not reported upstream. Root cause resolved 2026-08-06: ms3's expansion arithmetic faithfully implements the DCML harmony annotation standard's minor-mode scale-degree convention (vii = natural subtonic in minor contexts, # required for the leading tone); the defective rows are corpus-side annotation errors relative to that convention. See "Root-cause verdict" above for the full chain, reproduction, and citations.

Updated 2026-08-07 with two additions, both consistent with that verdict and neither implicating ms3 code: a proposed five-position displacement class was tested and ruled out (the case is a seven-position one), and a separate class of disagreement was recorded in which the expansion re-spells the staff without displacing any member, on which the annotation guidelines are silent. Nothing here has been reported upstream, and no upstream issue or Pull Request has been filed.
